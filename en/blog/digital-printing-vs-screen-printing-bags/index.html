Digital Printing vs Screen Printing for Custom Bags

Published by Xuhong Bags | 2026-06-23

A comprehensive comparison of six custom bag printing methods — screen printing, digital/DTG, DTF, sublimation, heat transfer, and embroidery — covering cost, durability, and best use cases for B2B buyers.

Your logo is the single most important element on a custom bag. But "print my logo" is not one thing — it is a choice among five or six different methods, each with different costs, durability, and visual results. Choosing the wrong method means a logo that cracks after three uses, looks washed out, or costs twice what it should.

This article walks through the six main printing methods for custom bags, with practical guidance for B2B buyers.

Screen Printing

How it works: Ink is pushed through a mesh screen onto the bag. Each color requires a separate screen. The ink sits on top of the fabric.

Best for:

  • Bold, simple logos with 1–4 solid colors
  • Text-heavy designs
  • Canvas and cotton bags
  • Orders of 500+ units

Cost: Screen setup costs $15–40 per screen (one-time). After setup, per-unit printing cost is low — often $0.10–0.30 per bag depending on color count and coverage.

Durability: Screen printing ink bonds into the fabric fibers. When cured correctly, a screen-printed logo lasts as long as the bag itself. It does not peel or crack under normal use and washing.

Limitations:

  • Not suitable for photographic or gradient images
  • Each additional color adds cost (more screens, more setup)
  • Small text under ~6pt may fill in
  • Large solid areas can feel plasticky if over-cured

B2B buyer rule of thumb: If your logo is a brand name in 1–2 colors, screen printing is almost always the best choice. It is the most cost-effective method at scale and the most durable.

Digital Printing (DTG — Direct-to-Garment)

How it works: A modified inkjet printer sprays ink directly onto the fabric, similar to printing on paper. No screens required.

Best for:

  • Photographic designs and complex gradients
  • Multi-color logos with fine details
  • Small orders where screen setup costs are not justified
  • Cotton canvas bags (works best on natural fibers)

Cost: No setup fee. Per-unit cost is higher than screen printing at scale — $0.50–1.50 per bag depending on print size. At 50–200 units, digital is often cheaper than screen printing because there is no screen setup cost. At 500+ units, screen printing usually beats digital on price.

Durability: Good, but not as durable as screen printing on canvas. Digital prints may fade slightly after 30–50 washes, especially on darker fabrics. A pre-treatment spray improves adhesion but adds cost.

Limitations:

  • Works best on light-colored, 100% cotton canvas
  • Polyester and dark fabrics are challenging without pre-treatment
  • Slower than screen printing for large orders
  • Not suitable for high-volume production runs (1,000+ pcs)

B2B buyer rule of thumb: Use digital printing for small-batch orders (under 300 pcs) with complex, full-color designs on light cotton canvas. For large orders with simple logos, screen printing wins on both cost and durability.

DTF Transfer (Direct-to-Film)

How it works: Design is printed onto a special film, then heat-pressed onto the bag. Works on cotton, polyester, and blends.

Best for:

  • Full-color, photographic designs
  • Small to medium orders (50–500 pcs)
  • Dark-colored bags where digital/DTG struggles
  • Bags made from blended or synthetic materials

Cost: Medium — $0.40–1.00 per transfer depending on size. No screen setup cost.

Durability: Good. DTF transfers have a slight hand feel (you can feel the print on the fabric surface). When pressed correctly, they withstand washing well but may show minor cracking at the edges after extended use.

Limitations:

  • Visible transfer edge on some fabric colors
  • Slightly plasticky feel compared to screen printing
  • Not as durable as screen printing for heavy-use bags
  • Slower per-unit than screen printing for large orders

B2B buyer rule of thumb: DTF is the practical middle ground — it handles full-color designs that screen printing cannot, at a lower per-unit cost than digital/DTG for medium-sized orders.

Sublimation Printing

How it works: Ink is turned into gas under heat and bonds into polyester fibers at a molecular level. The print becomes part of the fabric — no raised surface.

Best for:

  • Edge-to-edge, full-coverage prints
  • Photographic and gradient designs
  • Polyester bags only (will not work on cotton)
  • Promotional bags where visual impact matters most

Cost: Medium-high. Requires specialized paper and heat press equipment. Per-unit cost varies by coverage area.

Durability: Excellent — because the ink bonds into the fiber, it cannot crack, peel, or fade in the same way surface prints can. Sublimated prints last the life of the bag.

Limitations:

  • Works only on polyester (or high-polyester blends). Cotton will not hold sublimation ink.
  • Best on white or light-colored polyester. Dark polyester requires a white base layer.
  • Higher setup cost than screen printing for simple designs
  • Not available at all factories — requires specialized equipment

B2B buyer rule of thumb: If you need a full-coverage photo print and your bag is polyester, sublimation is the only method that delivers the result. If your bag is cotton, sublimation is not an option — use DTF or digital instead.

Heat Transfer

How it works: A pre-printed design on transfer paper is heat-pressed onto the bag. Similar to DTF but typically used for simpler designs and faster turnaround.

Best for:

  • Simple one-color logos
  • Rush orders with tight deadlines
  • Promotional bags where durability is less critical

Cost: Low. $0.10–0.30 per transfer.

Durability: Fair to good. Heat transfer prints can crack or peel after repeated washing, especially on bags that flex and fold.

Limitations:

  • Least durable method for bags that will be washed
  • May show a glossy "window" around the print on some fabrics
  • Not recommended for retail or premium bags

B2B buyer rule of thumb: Use heat transfer only when cost is the primary concern and the bag is for short-term use (events, giveaways, one-time promotions).

Embroidery

How it works: Thread is stitched directly into the bag fabric to form the logo.

Best for:

  • Premium corporate gifts
  • Small, detailed logos (brand marks, monograms)
  • Canvas and heavy cotton bags
  • Bags where perceived quality matters more than cost

Cost: Medium-high. $0.50–2.50 per logo depending on stitch count and complexity.

Durability: Excellent. Embroidery is the most durable logo application. The thread is physically stitched into the fabric and will not peel, crack, or fade.

Limitations:

  • Small text under ~8pt is hard to read
  • Fine gradients and photos are impossible
  • Large embroidered areas make the bag stiff
  • Higher per-unit cost than all printing methods
  • Slower production than screen printing

B2B buyer rule of thumb: Embroidery is for premium. A corporate gift tote with an embroidered logo communicates a different level of quality than a printed one. Use it selectively — for small, clean logos on high-value orders.

Common Mistakes When Choosing a Printing Method

Mistake 1: Ordering sublimation on cotton bags. Sublimation only works on polyester. If your bag is cotton and you want a full-coverage print, you need DTF or digital — not sublimation.

Mistake 2: Screen printing a 6-color photographic logo. Screen printing charges per color. A 6-color photo-quality print via screen printing will cost significantly more and look worse than the same design via DTF or digital. Use screen printing for 1–4 solid colors.

Mistake 3: Using heat transfer for retail bags. A heat transfer that peels after 5 washes makes your brand look cheap. For retail and premium bags, use screen printing, DTF, or embroidery.

Mistake 4: Not specifying ink type for polyester screen printing. Polyester requires different ink and lower curing temperature than cotton. If your factory prints both materials, confirm they use the right ink for your order.

Mistake 5: Assuming "digital" means the same thing everywhere. Some factories say "digital printing" to mean DTG, others mean DTF, and some mean UV digital printing on non-woven material. Always confirm the specific process, not just the category name.

FAQ

What is the best printing method for canvas tote bags? For 1–4 color logos on 500+ units, screen printing is the best combination of durability and cost. For full-color or photographic designs under 300 units, DTF or digital (DTG) are better options. For premium corporate gifts, embroidery delivers the highest perceived quality.

How long does screen printing last on bags? When properly cured, screen printing lasts the life of the bag. The ink bonds into the fabric fibers and does not crack or peel under normal use and washing.

Can you print full-color photos on canvas bags? Yes. DTF transfer and digital (DTG) printing both produce full-color photographic results on canvas. DTF works on dark and light fabrics; DTG works best on light-colored 100% cotton.

What is the cheapest printing method for bulk bag orders? Screen printing is the cheapest at scale (500+ units), with per-unit costs of $0.10–0.30 for simple designs. Heat transfer is slightly cheaper per-unit but significantly less durable — only use it for short-term promotional bags.

Does digital printing fade after washing? Digital (DTG) prints may show gradual fading after 30–50 washes. Pre-treatment improves adhesion. For bags meant for daily use and repeated washing, screen printing offers better wash durability.

Is embroidery better than screen printing? Embroidery is more durable and looks more premium, but costs 3–5× more per logo and cannot reproduce fine text or gradients. Use embroidery for small, clean logos on high-value orders; use screen printing for most standard B2B bag orders.
