OEM vs ODM Bags: What Is the Difference?

Published by Xuhong Bags | 2026-06-23

A clear explanation of OEM and ODM bag manufacturing with real examples, a side-by-side comparison, and a practical framework to help B2B buyers choose the right production model.

If you have started contacting bag manufacturers, you have probably seen "OEM" and "ODM" thrown around. Some factories say they do OEM. Others say ODM. Some say both. And if this is your first time sourcing custom bags, the difference might not be clear — but it matters because it affects your cost, your timeline, your MOQ, and how much control you have over the final product.

This article explains OEM vs ODM for bag manufacturing with real examples, no jargon.

OEM Bags: You Bring the Design

OEM stands for Original Equipment Manufacturing. In the bag world, it means: you provide the design, the factory produces it.

You send a tech pack with:

  • Bag dimensions (height × width × gusset depth)
  • Material specification (canvas weight, polyester denier, lining type)
  • Handle type and length
  • Logo placement and printing method
  • Zipper type, hardware finish
  • Packaging requirements

The factory sources the materials, cuts the fabric to your measurements, applies your logo, and ships the finished bags — all to your specification.

OEM is the right choice when:

  • You have a specific bag design in mind
  • You want to control every detail — size, material, hardware, stitching
  • You are building a brand with a distinct product identity
  • You have a tech pack or detailed reference photos

The trade-offs:

  • Higher MOQ (typically 500–1,000+ units for fully custom bags)
  • Longer lead time (sampling 10–20 days, production 25–40 days)
  • Higher per-unit cost due to custom material procurement and setup
  • You need to know what you want — the factory will build what you specify, not make design suggestions

Example: A skincare brand wants a custom canvas tote with a specific size (38cm × 32cm × 12cm gusset), organic 12oz cotton, leather handles, a magnetic snap closure, an inside zipper pocket, and their logo embroidered on the front. This is an OEM project — every element is specified by the brand.

ODM Bags: The Factory Brings the Design

ODM stands for Original Design Manufacturing. In the bag world, it means: the factory has existing bag designs, and you select one, customize the logo/color/material, and order it under your brand.

The factory has a catalog of bag styles they have already developed — proven patterns, tested materials, known production costs. You browse the catalog, pick a style, choose your preferred material and color, add your logo, and the factory produces it.

ODM is the right choice when:

  • You do not have a specific bag design — you just know you need "canvas tote bags with my logo"
  • You want lower MOQ (300–500 units is common on ODM templates)
  • You need faster turnaround (sampling 5–7 days, production 15–25 days)
  • Your budget is tighter — ODM uses existing patterns and standard materials, so costs are lower
  • You are new to bag sourcing and want a proven design that works

The trade-offs:

  • Less design control — you work within the factory's existing patterns
  • Your bag may look similar to other brands using the same ODM template
  • Hardware, handle styles, and construction details are pre-determined

Example: A startup coffee roaster wants branded tote bags for their retail stores. They do not have a specific bag design in mind. They look at a factory's ODM catalog, choose a 12oz canvas tote template in natural color with long handles, add their logo via screen printing, and order 500 units. This is an ODM project — the bag design existed before the brand came along.

The Hybrid Reality: Most Orders Are Somewhere in Between

In practice, many B2B bag orders are not pure OEM or pure ODM — they fall somewhere on a spectrum:

ODM with modifications: You start with a factory's existing tote bag template, but you change the handle length, add an inside pocket, and switch the material from standard cotton to recycled canvas. The factory adjusts the pattern and quotes accordingly. This is the most common arrangement for small to mid-size brands — you get the speed and cost benefits of ODM with some customization.

OEM-lite: You send a photo of a bag you like and say "make something like this, but in 14oz canvas with these dimensions." The factory adapts an existing pattern rather than building from scratch. This is faster and cheaper than pure OEM but gives you more control than pure ODM.

When contacting a manufacturer, be specific about where you fall on this spectrum. "I want your ODM tote #T03, but with 12oz washed canvas instead of the standard 10oz, and 5cm longer handles" is a clear, actionable request. "I want OEM bags" without a tech pack is not.

What About White Label and Private Label?

These terms sometimes appear alongside OEM/ODM and are worth clarifying:

White label: Generic products produced by one company and sold under another company's brand. The product is not exclusive — multiple brands may sell the same bag with different logos. In bag manufacturing, white label is essentially ODM: you pick from existing designs.

Private label: A product manufactured by a third party and sold exclusively under your brand. The product is unique to you — no other brand can buy the same bag. Private label can be OEM (you design it) or ODM (factory designs it exclusively for you under contract).

For most B2B bag buyers, the practical question is not "OEM or ODM" in the abstract, but "how much customization do I need and how much am I willing to pay for it?"

How to Decide: A Practical Framework

Ask yourself three questions:

1. Do I have a specific design in mind, or do I just need quality bags with my logo? Specific design → OEM. Quality bags with logo → ODM.

2. What is my order quantity? Under 500 units → ODM is usually the only practical option. 500–1,000 units → You have a choice. ODM gives better pricing; OEM gives more control. 1,000+ units → OEM becomes the standard approach.

3. How fast do I need the bags? Under 4 weeks → ODM is your only realistic option. 4–8 weeks → OEM/ODM hybrid is possible. 8+ weeks → Full OEM is workable.

Common Mistakes When Choosing Between OEM and ODM

Mistake 1: Asking for "OEM" without a design. If you do not have a tech pack or reference spec, you do not need OEM — you need ODM. The factory cannot build something from nothing. Start with ODM templates and customize from there.

Mistake 2: Expecting ODM pricing for a fully custom OEM bag. An ODM bag costs less because the pattern exists, the material is in stock, and the production line knows the process. An OEM bag costs more at every step. Do not expect ODM pricing for a bag designed from scratch.

Mistake 3: Assuming OEM means "better quality." Quality depends on the factory's standards, materials, and QC process — not whether the design is OEM or ODM. An ODM bag from a quality factory will be better than an OEM bag from a factory with poor QC.

Mistake 4: Over-customizing an ODM template until it becomes OEM. Every modification adds cost and time. Changing material, size, handle, closure, and adding pockets to an ODM template effectively turns it into an OEM project. Be clear-eyed about what each change costs.

Mistake 5: Not asking what "OEM" and "ODM" mean to the specific factory. Different factories use these terms differently. Some call any custom-logo order "OEM." Others only use OEM for full custom designs. Always describe what you want in plain terms — do not rely on the label alone.

FAQ

What is the difference between OEM and ODM bags? OEM means you provide the design and the factory produces it to your specification. ODM means the factory has existing bag designs, and you select one to customize with your logo, color, and material preferences. OEM gives more control but costs more and requires higher MOQ; ODM is faster and cheaper but limits design options.

Which is cheaper — OEM or ODM bag production? ODM is cheaper because the factory uses existing patterns, standard materials, and known production processes. OEM requires custom pattern development, special material procurement, and production line adjustment — all of which add cost. A 12oz cotton tote might cost $1.20–1.80 via ODM and $1.80–2.50 via OEM for the same order quantity.

Do I need my own design to order custom bags? No. If you do not have a specific design, ODM is the right choice. Browse the factory's existing templates, choose one that fits your needs, and customize the color, material, and logo. If you have a specific design, OEM is the right model.

What is white label vs private label for bags? White label means a generic bag sold under multiple brands with different logos. Private label means a bag manufactured exclusively for your brand. White label is essentially ODM; private label can be OEM or ODM depending on your arrangement with the factory.

How long does OEM bag production take? Full OEM typically takes 10–20 days for sampling and 25–40 days for production, for a total of 5–9 weeks. ODM is faster: 5–7 days for sampling and 15–25 days for production, totaling 3–5 weeks. Shipping time is additional.

Can I modify an ODM bag design? Yes. Most ODM templates can be modified — different material, handle length, pocket addition, closure change. Each modification adds cost and may increase lead time. Heavy modification of an ODM template effectively turns it into an OEM project.
