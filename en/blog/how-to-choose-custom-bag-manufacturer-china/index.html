How to Choose a Custom Bag Manufacturer in China

Published by Xuhong Bags | 2026-06-23

A practical 6-step guide for B2B buyers evaluating bag manufacturers in China — covering factory verification, sampling, MOQ, QC inspection, payment terms, and communication red flags.

Finding a bag manufacturer in China is not hard. Finding one that delivers consistent quality, communicates clearly, and does not disappear after your first order — that takes a process.

This guide is for B2B buyers who have never ordered custom bags from China, as well as experienced importers who want a checklist they can use tomorrow. No theory. Just what works.

Step 1: Know What Kind of Manufacturer You Need

Not all bag factories are the same. Before you send a single inquiry, figure out which category fits your order.

Template-based factories work from existing bag patterns. They have standard sizes, standard materials, and standard processes. MOQ is lower (often 300–500 pcs), sampling is faster, and pricing is more predictable. This is the right choice if you are ordering canvas tote bags, drawstring bags, or basic cosmetic pouches with your logo.

Full-custom factories build from your tech pack. You send a spec sheet with bag dimensions, material, lining, zipper type, handle style, printing method, and packaging. MOQ is higher (usually 1,000+ pcs), sampling takes longer, and the per-unit cost is higher — but you get exactly what you designed.

Trading companies do not own a factory. They source from multiple workshops, mark up the price, and handle communication and logistics. Trading companies can be useful for small, complex orders spanning multiple product types, but you pay 10–30% more than working directly with a factory.

If you are ordering 1,000+ standard canvas tote bags with a logo, a template-based factory is almost always the best value.

Step 2: Verify the Manufacturer Before You Send Money

Three checks that take 30 minutes and can save you from a bad order:

1. Business license check. Ask for the factory's business license. A real factory will have production or manufacturing in its registered business scope. A trading company will only have trading. Free databases like tianyancha.com let you verify this in minutes.

2. Video call with the production floor. Ask for a live video walkthrough — not pre-recorded footage. You want to see sewing lines, material storage, QC stations, and finished goods. If they refuse or only send photos, that is a signal.

3. Ask for a recent client reference. A factory that has done work for overseas brands should be able to name one or two clients (with permission) or show recent shipment photos with shipping marks visible.

Step 3: Understand the Sampling Process

Sampling is the most important step in custom bag manufacturing — and the one most first-time buyers rush.

Pre-production sample (PPS): One or two bags made to your exact specifications before bulk production starts. This is your opportunity to check material, color, printing quality, stitching, size, and handle attachment. You sign off on the PPS, and bulk production must match it.

Normal sampling timeline:

  • Standard template with existing material: 5–7 days
  • Custom design with new material: 10–15 days
  • Complex bag with multiple compartments/lining: 15–25 days

Sample fee: Most factories charge a sample fee ($50–150 for standard bags, more for complex designs). A reputable factory will credit the sample fee toward your bulk order. If a factory offers "free samples" with no conditions, be cautious — the sample may be a generic bag they photographed well, not one made to your spec.

What to check on your sample:

  • Material weight and feel — does it match what you discussed?
  • Stitch density — 8–10 stitches per inch is standard for canvas bags
  • Print alignment — is the logo centered, straight, and clean at the edges?
  • Handle attachment — bartack or cross-stitch? Bartack is stronger.
  • Color accuracy — did they match your Pantone or just approximate?

Step 4: Understand MOQ and Pricing Structure

MOQ (minimum order quantity) varies by material, complexity, and the factory's production model.

Typical MOQ ranges:

  • Standard canvas tote with one logo placement: 300–500 pcs
  • Custom size/material canvas tote: 500–1,000 pcs
  • Drawstring bags: 300–500 pcs
  • Complex bags with lining, zippers, multiple compartments: 1,000+ pcs

If a factory quotes MOQ 100 pcs for a fully custom bag, they are likely a trading company aggregating small orders or cutting corners on quality. Low MOQ on standard templates is normal; low MOQ on full custom is a red flag.

Step 5: Quality Control — Do Not Skip This

Three levels of QC, from minimum to maximum:

Level 1: Factory self-inspection (minimum). The factory checks its own output. Acceptable for repeat orders with a trusted factory, but not for a first order.

Level 2: Third-party inspection (recommended). Hire an inspection company (SGS, Bureau Veritas, AsiaInspection, etc.) to check your goods before shipment. Cost: $200–400 per inspection day. They check quantity, workmanship, function, packaging, and loading. This is the standard approach for most B2B buyers.

Level 3: Your own QC on site (maximum). Send your own person to the factory during production. Only practical for large, ongoing orders.

For a first order from a new factory, Level 2 is the minimum sensible approach. The $300 inspection fee is cheap insurance against receiving a container of bags with crooked logos.

When to inspect:

  • Inline inspection: During production (30–50% complete). Catches systemic problems early.
  • Final random inspection (FRI): After production, before shipment. Industry standard is AQL 2.5 (no more than 2.5% defects in the sample).

Step 6: Communication Red Flags

These signals suggest the factory may not be a good long-term partner:

  • Takes more than 24–48 hours to reply during working days. A factory with good English-speaking sales staff should respond within one business day.
  • Answers are vague. "We can do any material" without asking about your specific use case is not helpful. Good sales staff ask clarifying questions before quoting.
  • Quotes come back too fast. A serious quote for a custom bag takes time — they need to calculate material consumption, printing cost, labor time, and packaging. A quote returned in 10 minutes is a rough guess, not a real price.
  • Won't provide a production timeline. You should know: sampling ETA, material procurement window, production start date, completion date, and shipping date.
  • Pushes for 100% payment upfront. Standard payment terms for custom bag orders: 30% deposit to start production, 70% before shipment (after inspection photos or report). 100% upfront from an unverified factory is a hard no.

FAQ

How do I find a reliable bag manufacturer in China? Start by identifying what type of manufacturer you need (template-based, full-custom, or trading company). Verify their business license, request a live video call of the production floor, and ask for recent client references. Avoid factories that refuse video calls or only send stock photos.

What should I check before placing a bulk order? Always get a pre-production sample and check: material weight and feel, stitch density (8–10 stitches/inch standard), logo alignment and print quality, handle attachment method, and color accuracy. Hire a third-party inspection for the first order with a new factory.

What is a normal MOQ for custom bags in China? Standard templates: 300–500 pcs. Custom size/material: 500–1,000 pcs. Complex bags with lining and multiple compartments: 1,000+ pcs. MOQ under 100 pcs for fully custom bags is unusual and may indicate a trading company or quality shortcuts.

How long does custom bag sampling take? Standard template with existing material: 5–7 days. Custom design with new material: 10–15 days. Complex multi-compartment bags: 15–25 days. Shipping time is additional.

Should I visit the factory before ordering? A factory visit is ideal but not always practical. A live video call covering the production floor, material storage, QC stations, and finished goods is an acceptable alternative for first orders. If the factory refuses both, do not proceed.

How do I handle quality control for custom bags? For a first order, hire a third-party inspection company (SGS, BV, AsiaInspection) for a final random inspection at AQL 2.5. Cost is $200–400. For ongoing orders, inline inspection during production catches problems early.
