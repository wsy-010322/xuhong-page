Custom Tote Bag MOQ Guide: What to Expect and How to Get a Lower MOQ

Published by Xuhong Bags | 2026-06-24

Learn typical MOQ for custom tote bags, what drives it up or down, and practical ways to get a lower minimum order without sacrificing quality.

MOQ — minimum order quantity — is often the first number buyers look for when contacting a custom bag factory. For small brands and startups, a high MOQ can be a dealbreaker. For larger buyers, understanding MOQ helps with budget planning and supply chain management.

This guide explains what drives MOQ in custom tote bag production, what typical MOQ ranges look like, and — most importantly — how to get a lower MOQ without compromising quality.

Why Do Bag Factories Have MOQ?

MOQ is not arbitrary. Every custom bag order requires the factory to spend time and money before the first bag is sewn. These setup costs are fixed — they don't change whether you order 50 bags or 5,000.

The main setup costs for a custom tote bag order include:

  • Material sourcing: Fabric mills also have MOQ. If your order uses 500 meters of canvas but the mill sells in 1,000-meter rolls, the factory either buys the minimum or pays a premium.
  • Cutting die preparation: Each bag shape needs a cutting die. A custom shape requires a new die; a standard template may already have one.
  • Screen preparation: Screen printing requires one screen per color. A 2-color design needs two screens, with setup labor for each.
  • Production line setup: Setting up sewing machines, training workers on the new design, and running quality checks all take time regardless of order size.

The factory needs enough units to absorb these fixed costs and still make production worthwhile. That's the minimum.

Typical MOQ Ranges for Custom Tote Bags

MOQ varies by material, customization level, and printing method. Here are the typical ranges:

ScenarioTypical MOQ
ODM template, single-color screen print100–300 pcs
ODM template, multi-color or digital print200–500 pcs
OEM custom design, standard material500–1,000 pcs
OEM custom design, special material or color1,000–2,000 pcs
Premium material (organic cotton, recycled PET, nylon)500–1,000+ pcs

These are mid-range factory estimates. A factory specializing in small orders may go lower. A large factory focused on volume may set higher minimums.

What Drives MOQ Up

Custom materials. If you want a specific fabric color that the factory doesn't stock, the mill MOQ applies — often 500 to 1,000 meters. That alone pushes the bag MOQ up.

Custom hardware. Custom zipper pulls, buckles, or rivets require molds. The mold cost is fixed, so small quantities are very expensive per bag.

Multiple colors or complex printing. Each screen for screen printing costs $30 to $80 to make. A 4-color print on a 100-piece order means the screen fee alone is $1.20 to $3.20 per bag — before any fabric or sewing cost.

Fully custom shape and size. A new cutting die costs $50 to $150. If your design is a non-standard size, the factory may need a dedicated cutting setup, increasing the fixed cost.

Low-volume packaging requirements. Individual retail-ready packaging (folding, hang tag, care label, barcode) adds labor. Small orders carry a higher per-unit labor cost.

How to Get a Lower MOQ — Without Sacrificing Quality

1. Start with ODM templates.

The single biggest MOQ reducer is using an existing factory template instead of a fully custom design. The factory already has the cutting die, sewing pattern, and material sourcing set up. You still get your logo and branding — just on a proven design. Many factories accept 50 to 100 pieces on ODM templates.

2. Choose standard materials and colors.

If you select a fabric the factory stocks regularly, there's no mill MOQ to meet. Ask the factory what materials and colors they carry in stock. A natural 12oz cotton canvas is widely available; a custom-dyed forest green 14oz organic cotton is not.

3. Keep printing simple.

Single-color screen printing on one side of the bag has the lowest setup cost. Every additional color adds a screen fee. Digital printing has no screen fee but a higher per-unit cost — which can be more economical for very small orders if your design is multi-color.

4. Order in quantity tiers.

Some factories offer tiered pricing with partial deliveries. For example, you commit to 1,000 pieces total but receive 300 pieces in month one, 300 in month two, and 400 in month three. The factory gets volume commitment; you get lower unit cost without storing all 1,000 bags at once.

5. Group multiple designs under one order.

If you need 200 tote bags in design A and 200 in design B, combining them into a 400-piece order on the same bag template (just different prints) may help meet a factory's minimum. The cutting and sewing are the same; only the screen changes between runs.

6. Ask about in-stock or overrun materials.

Sometimes a factory has leftover fabric from a previous large order. If you are flexible on color, using in-stock material can bypass the mill MOQ entirely and lower your bag MOQ.

7. Consider split production.

The same bag body with different prints counts toward the total quantity. If you need 100 bags each with 5 different logo variations, that is still 500 bags of the same cut-and-sew — the factory may treat it as a 500-piece order for production and apply only screen change fees between runs.

MOQ Is Negotiable — With the Right Approach

MOQ is rarely set in stone. A factory that quotes 500 pieces may accept 300 if you:

  • Use a standard template and in-stock material
  • Pay slightly more per unit to cover the fixed costs on a smaller run
  • Commit to a larger reorder within a set timeframe
  • Send the order during a slower production season

The key is understanding what drives MOQ and offering the factory something that makes the smaller run worthwhile.

Common Misunderstandings About MOQ

"Higher MOQ always means lower unit price." True up to a point. Beyond 5,000 to 10,000 pieces, the unit cost savings per additional thousand become small. The biggest price drop happens between 100 and 1,000 pieces.

"All factories have the same MOQ." Factory size, specialization, and target market affect MOQ significantly. A small workshop may accept 50 pieces. A large OEM factory may not take orders below 2,000. Ask multiple factories.

"Low MOQ means a bad factory." Not necessarily. Some factories specialize in small-batch production for startup brands. They charge a higher unit price to cover costs but deliver good quality. High MOQ does not automatically mean better quality, just larger scale.

FAQ

What is the typical MOQ for custom tote bags? ODM template bags: 100–300 pieces. OEM custom designs: 500–1,000 pieces. Some factories accept as low as 50 pieces on standard templates with simple printing.

Why do custom bag factories have MOQ? Fixed setup costs — material sourcing, cutting dies, screen preparation, and production line setup — must be spread across enough units to make the order economical for the factory.

How can I get a lower MOQ on custom tote bags? Use ODM templates, choose standard materials and colors, keep printing simple, group multiple prints on one template, or ask about in-stock/overrun materials.

Does higher MOQ always mean lower unit price? The biggest price drop happens between 100 and 1,000 pieces. Beyond 5,000–10,000, additional savings per thousand units become small.

Is it possible to order 50 custom tote bags? Yes, if you use an ODM template with standard material and simple single-color printing. Expect a higher unit cost than orders of 500+ pieces, because setup costs are spread across fewer units.

What is a reasonable MOQ for a startup brand? 100 to 300 pieces on ODM templates is a practical starting point. It is enough to test the market without overcommitting on inventory while keeping the unit cost manageable.
