Cotton vs Polyester Tote Bags: Which Is Better for Your Brand?

Published by Xuhong Bags | 2026-06-23

A side-by-side comparison of cotton and polyester materials for custom tote bag orders — covering durability, printing, cost, eco-friendliness, and recommended choices by use case.

Every B2B buyer ordering custom bags eventually hits the same question: cotton or polyester? Both are mainstream materials, both have strong arguments, and both can be right — depending on what your bag needs to do. The wrong choice means a bag that looks cheap, wears out too fast, or costs more than it should.

This article breaks down cotton and polyester for custom tote bags side by side, covering what actually matters for B2B orders: durability, printing, cost, shipping, and brand perception.

When Cotton (Canvas) Is the Better Choice

Cotton canvas is the default choice when the bag is part of the brand experience — when the customer is meant to keep it and use it repeatedly.

Cotton works best for:

  • Retail shopping bags. A boutique or bookstore wants the bag to feel like a product, not a disposable wrapper. Cotton conveys quality before the customer reads the logo.
  • Corporate gifts. You are giving the bag as a gift. It should feel substantial. A 12oz or 14oz cotton tote in someone's hand says "this company cares about details."
  • Premium brand packaging. Luxury brands often choose heavy cotton canvas with a magnetic snap or zipper — the bag becomes part of the product presentation.
  • Screen printing with fine details. Cotton canvas provides a stable, flat surface for multi-color screen printing with tight registration.

The trade-off: Cotton bags are heavier to ship and cost more per unit. If your order is 5,000 units going by sea freight, the weight difference versus polyester is real money.

When Polyester Is the Better Choice

Polyester tote bags dominate two scenarios: large-quantity promotional orders and any use case where the bag needs to be lightweight, foldable, and water-resistant.

Polyester works best for:

  • Trade show and event giveaways. You need 2,000 bags that cost under $1 each, fold small enough to stuff into a conference tote, and survive one or two uses before the attendee goes home.
  • Sports and outdoor events. Polyester dries fast, does not mildew, and handles rain without turning into a wet rag.
  • Grocery and shopping bags in wet climates. In markets where rain is common, polyester bags are more practical than cotton — they can be wiped dry.
  • Sublimation printing. If you need a full-coverage, edge-to-edge photo-quality print, polyester is the only material that takes sublimation. Cotton requires a different process (DTF or screen print) and cannot achieve the same all-over coverage.

The trade-off: Polyester feels cheaper in hand. No amount of printing makes a 70D polyester bag feel like a 12oz cotton bag. If brand perception matters more than unit cost, polyester is the wrong call.

Understanding Polyester Fabric Types: Taffeta vs Oxford

Not all "polyester bags" are the same. The two main polyester fabric types used in bag manufacturing are polyester taffeta and polyester oxford. Knowing the difference helps you specify exactly what you want.

Polyester Taffeta

A plain-weave, smooth, lightweight polyester fabric. Measured by T count — the total number of warp + weft threads per square inch. Higher T = denser, more durable fabric.

Common T-count specifications (standard stock):

T CountWeightTypical Use
170T~55 g/m²Ultra-thin, cheap lining, simple packaging
180T~62 g/m²Garment linings, umbrella base fabric
190T~67 g/m²General-purpose: bag linings, down jacket shells, rain covers
210T~78 g/m²Most common: backpack linings, sun protection, tents, dust covers
230T~85 g/m²Thicker: outdoor waterproof uses, bag interlining

For custom bags, 190T and 210T are the most frequently ordered specifications. 170T is too thin for anything that needs to hold shape. 230T adds cost with diminishing returns for most bag applications.

Taffeta is best for: lightweight drawstring bags, foldable shopping bags, bag linings, promotional tote bags that need to pack flat.

Polyester Oxford

A basket-weave fabric with a more textured, durable surface. Measured by denier (D) — the weight in grams of 9,000 meters of a single fiber. Higher D = thicker, heavier, more abrasion-resistant.

Common denier specifications:

DenierFeelTypical Use
210DLight, budgetStorage bags, backpack linings, tent inner layers
300DLight-mediumBag interlining, waterproof base fabric
420DMedium, abrasion-resistantCasual bags, travel bags, pet bags
600DUniversal workhorseBackpacks, outdoor bags, tent outer fabric — the industry standard
840DThick, heavy-dutyTool bags, heavy-duty backpacks
1200D/1680DUltra-thick, extreme durabilityLuggage, military bags, heavy outdoor gear

Two material variants:

  • Polyester oxford: Affordable, stiff, abrasion-resistant. The mainstream choice for bags.
  • Nylon oxford: Softer, higher tensile strength, higher price. Used for premium bags where weight-to-strength ratio matters.

For most B2B custom bag orders, 420D and 600D polyester oxford cover the vast majority of use cases. 210D is for lightweight economy bags; 840D+ is for specialized heavy-duty applications.

Polyester-Cotton Blend (TC Fabric): A Practical Middle Ground

If you want to reduce cost while keeping a fabric feel closer to cotton, a polyester-cotton blend (TC fabric) is worth considering. Common blends are 65% polyester / 35% cotton (T/C 65/35) and 80/20.

TC fabric advantages:

  • Costs 20–30% less than pure cotton canvas
  • Wrinkles less than 100% cotton
  • Dries faster, less prone to mildew
  • Still takes screen printing (though adhesion is slightly weaker than on pure cotton)

TC fabric trade-offs:

  • Does not have the same natural hand feel as 100% cotton
  • Not biodegradable — the polyester component remains
  • Screen printing requires slightly different curing parameters

TC fabric is a smart choice for mid-range promotional bags, corporate giveaways where budget matters but you do not want the synthetic feel of pure polyester, and retail bags for brands that prioritize cost over premium perception.

For most B2B orders with a standard logo (1–3 colors, no full coverage), cotton screen printing is the most reliable combination. The result is consistent, durable, and cost-effective at scale.

For all-over photo prints or gradient-heavy designs, polyester sublimation is essentially the only option that delivers the result buyers expect.

Cost Comparison: Cotton vs Polyester

As a general reference for B2B orders (prices vary by design, quantity, and current material market):

  • A basic 80g/m² polyester drawstring bag is typically 30–50% cheaper than a 10oz cotton tote bag of similar size.
  • A 12oz cotton tote is typically 2–3× the fabric cost of a standard polyester tote.
  • However, polyester sublimation printing adds cost — a full-coverage sublimated polyester bag can approach the price of a screen-printed 12oz cotton tote.

The cheapest option overall: a simple polyester bag with a one-color screen print. The most premium option: a 14oz cotton canvas tote with multi-color screen print and embroidered accent.

Eco-Friendliness: What Buyers Actually Care About

B2B buyers increasingly ask about sustainability, but the conversation is more nuanced than "cotton good, polyester bad."

  • Cotton is a natural fiber and biodegradable — but conventional cotton farming uses significant water and pesticides. Organic cotton and recycled cotton address some of these concerns at a higher cost.
  • Polyester is plastic-based and does not biodegrade, but rPET (recycled polyester made from plastic bottles) is widely available and has a lower initial carbon footprint than virgin cotton.

If your end customer values "natural materials," cotton is the clear winner. If they value "made from recycled materials," rPET polyester can be a strong sustainability story. Be specific about what your bag is made from — vague claims like "eco-friendly bag" without detail do not help buyers or end consumers.

Common Mistakes When Choosing Between Cotton and Polyester

Mistake 1: Choosing polyester to save cost, then printing a brand logo that looks washed out. Polyester screen printing requires different ink and curing than cotton. A factory that specializes in cotton may produce subpar polyester prints if not asked specifically.

Mistake 2: Choosing cotton for a one-time event. If the bag will be used once and discarded, 12oz cotton is overkill and over-budget. 8oz cotton or polyester both work better for single-use events.

Mistake 3: Not confirming the polyester spec. "Polyester bag" can mean polyester taffeta (measured in T count: 170T–230T), polyester oxford (measured in denier: 210D–1680D), non-woven polypropylene, or nylon oxford. Each has a completely different feel, weight, and cost. Always confirm the T count or denier spec in your quote — 70D taffeta and 600D oxford are entirely different fabrics.

Mistake 4: Assuming cotton is automatically "eco-friendly." Conventional cotton has a significant environmental footprint. If sustainability is a selling point for your brand, ask about organic cotton, recycled cotton, or GOTS-certified options — and be prepared for a higher unit cost.

FAQ

Are cotton tote bags better than polyester? It depends on the use case. Cotton feels more premium and prints better with screen printing. Polyester is lighter, cheaper, water-resistant, and the only option for sublimation printing. For retail and corporate use, cotton usually wins. For events and bulk giveaways, polyester is more practical.

Which is more durable — cotton or polyester tote bags? Both are durable in different ways. Cotton canvas (especially 12oz+) handles heavy loads and abrasion well but degrades if repeatedly soaked in water. Polyester handles water, UV, and mildew better but can tear more easily at the seams under sharp weight.

Can polyester bags be printed with logos? Yes. Screen printing, heat transfer, and sublimation all work on polyester. Sublimation is polyester-only and produces the best full-coverage results. Screen printing on polyester requires different ink and curing temperature than cotton — confirm your manufacturer handles both.

Are cotton tote bags eco-friendly? Cotton is biodegradable and made from natural fibers, but conventional cotton farming uses significant water and pesticides. Organic cotton and recycled cotton reduce the environmental impact. rPET polyester made from recycled bottles is another option if sustainability matters to your brand.

What is cheaper — cotton or polyester custom bags? Polyester is typically 30–50% cheaper than cotton for comparable bag sizes. A basic polyester tote might cost $0.50–0.80 at bulk quantity, while a 12oz cotton tote with equivalent printing might cost $1.20–2.00. Actual pricing depends on design, quantity, and printing requirements.

Which material is better for outdoor or event use? Polyester. It dries fast, resists mildew, and can be wiped clean. For outdoor festivals, sports events, or rainy climates, polyester is the practical choice over cotton.
